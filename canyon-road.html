Laguna Canyon · Field notes

Laguna Canyon Road, mile by mile

From El Toro Road to downtown, one photograph at a time.

Most of us know Laguna Canyon Road as the drive in and out of town. In November 2026 I drove the whole road slowly and photographed what sits along it. What you see is less a highway than a neighborhood: open space, animal care, workshops, artists, an art college and, at the bottom, the festivals that made Laguna an art town.

The photographs run in order, top of the canyon to the bottom, in four stretches. Tap any photo to see it larger.

Upper canyon

From El Toro Road, the canyon starts as open space: the Berns canyon preserve, then a run of places that care for animals, including an animal hospital, the city shelter, the marine mammal center and the dog park. The food pantry, self-storage and the new ArtLofts studios share the stretch.

27 photographs

Big Bend

Around the bend, the canyon goes to work. Upholsterers, contractor yards, a body shop, a food company, framers and a skimboard maker. The Laguna Canyon Artists studios are here, and so is the biggest change on the road: Laguna College of Art & Design’s new Big Bend campus, under construction.

36 photographs

Lower canyon corridor

The stretch most locals know. Studios and small shops behind roll-up doors, a race shop, a winery, lumber and building-material yards, towing and smog checks, with the art college’s main campus in the middle of it all. It is Laguna’s working neighborhood, the place where things get made and fixed.

8 photographs

Where the canyon meets town

The canyon opens toward downtown with the Boys & Girls Club and the grounds of Laguna’s summer art festivals: the Sawdust, the Festival of Arts and Art-A-Fair.

The takeaway

Drive it slowly and the canyon reads as Laguna’s working neighborhood: the place where things get made, fixed, stored and taught. Very little of it would fit anywhere else in town, which is exactly why it matters. More on Laguna Canyon →
